# Index ordering stability audit

Audit date: 21 October 2026. Baseline: [source-notes.md](source-notes.md), observed on 6 October 2026. Scope: all 42 IDs in [seed_website_indexes.sql](../sql/seed_website_indexes.sql), including the four currently inactive entries: 8, 14, 25 and 39.

## Summary

**Stopping at the first previously seen article is unsafe across this catalogue.** Newly appearing links occur below shared, already known links at Coliban Water, Gippsland Water's current-projects listing, North East Water, Wannon Water, Goulburn-Murray Water and Joint Treaties. These are counterexamples to a universal prefix-only insertion rule. A newly appearing link does not, by itself, prove a newly published article.

The checked baseline contains specific ordering or structural findings for 15 indexes. Archive evidence is **consistent for nine**, **inconclusive for six**, and establishes **no different verdict**. The other **27 have no specific prior ordering finding**; that label describes the baseline, not archive success or stability. None of the 42 is certified safe for future early stopping.

CES news shows a dated new publication entering at the front in the sampled first-page comparison. Infrastructure shows prefix arrivals ordered by opening date within its open section. Engage Victoria also has a prefix-only arrival sample. These observations do not guarantee future stability, resolve ties or establish coverage of all pagination.

## Methodology

1. Read the local seed, source notes and discovery routing. Preserve the seed's IDs, URL identity and inactive status. Treat the existing notes as a dated baseline, not repeated-run evidence. Broad remarks about curated collections are not individualized findings.
2. Use [Wayback CDX](https://github.com/internetarchive/wayback/tree/master/wayback-cdx-server) JSON metadata, requesting timestamp, original URL, status, MIME type and digest. Search successful HTML captures in the bounded 21 October 2025–21 October 2026 window. Metadata for that whole window was retrieved once per URL; replay candidates were inspected newest first, expanding into older available captures when recent comparisons lacked evidence. This used a single bounded metadata query rather than separate recent/90-day/year queries.
3. Inspect up to eight primary seed-page candidate times, avoiding adjacent identical digests. Stop when a usable comparison has an addition and multiple shared items, or the available candidates/budget are exhausted. This finds a short available informative comparison within inspected candidates, **not a proven globally shortest comparison**. Verify suspicious results and follow archived project hubs, bills, section filters and pagination where metadata and replays permit.
4. Additional downstream listings received at most two selected captures per route; recovered Southern Rural Water metadata supplied three. Verification retries reused selected times. Consequently, the eight-candidate cap was enforced on primary seed pages rather than all downstream timestamps combined; some indexes exceed eight distinct times across their followed routes. This is a limitation against a strict per-index interpretation of the planned cap.
5. Fetch archived HTML only, blocking redirects outside Internet Archive. Decode archived compression, strip replay wrappers from link identities, and examine scoped article links, headings, explicit dates, project status and featured/pinned markers. Inspect server-embedded data where available: Engage's Inertia project array and the bills page's Nuxt configuration. Do not call live search APIs or load live JavaScript to repair missing archive content.
6. Compare item positions and the relative sequence of shared identities within each section. Normalize demonstrated scheme/www/trailing-slash aliases; do not call layout aliases new articles. Distinguish publication, last-updated, referral, opening and project-lifespan dates. A digest change alone proves no ordering change.
7. Pace archive requests through a shared gate, at least 1.1 seconds between request starts initially and 1.2 seconds for follow-up work. Use bounded attempts (normally an initial request plus one retry, with backoff); count selected capture times separately from HTTP attempts. Treat connection refusals, HTTP errors and incomplete dynamic content as failures/limitations, never empty listings.
8. Use [Availability](https://archive.org/help/wayback_api.php) for fallback discovery. Most fallback calls returned HTTP 429. Its closest result is not necessarily inside the requested period: DCCEEW's April 2024 result was excluded. CDX metadata alone does not mean a replay was readable.

Timestamps below are **actual replay capture timestamps in UTC**, not article dates. Wayback sometimes substituted another capture: the Assembly's requested May capture replayed February, and Infrastructure's requested September capture replayed April. Such substitutions were not counted as independent evidence. Links marked CDX are reproducible metadata queries, **not inspected article snapshots**.

### Verdict meanings

- **Consistent:** inspected evidence supports a specific baseline ordering/structural observation, sometimes only part of it. It does not prove stability.
- **Different:** comparable inspected evidence contradicts a specific baseline observation. No such verdict was established here.
- **Inconclusive:** a specific prior finding exists, but relevant archived content or comparisons cannot verify it.
- **No specific prior finding:** no usable individual ordering finding exists in the checked notes. The archive result is stated separately.

## Limitations and practical implication

This is a bounded archive audit, not a reconstruction of every historical run. Missing successful metadata does not prove a site was never archived. HTTP 200 can contain an incomplete shell; a successful old replay does not guarantee repeat access, as some verification retries failed. Explicit empty consultation states at CER are distinguished from those failures. Snapshot timestamps across linked sections/pages differ, so they cannot be presented as an atomic historical view.

No complete browser-rendered pagination was reconstructed. Coliban's load-more state is partial; CES has only one inspected historical second page; Infrastructure's selected next-page query returned no captures; Engage's server payload shows extensive pagination without an inspected page-two counterpart. Server-array order is evidence of the archived supplied data, not proof that all client-side display logic ran unchanged. Featured/pinned behavior could only be checked where captured content exposed it; East Gippsland's known featured exception remains unverified.

Prefer complete eligible-section/page discovery followed by URL membership checks over stopping at the first previously successful URL. Stable inline pages and documents require separate content/version refresh logic. Even a publication-descending feed can have ties, removals, backfill or later policy changes. These recommendations follow the inspected counterexamples and coverage limits; they do not change scraper behavior.
